Loudspeakers

Discover the fascinating world of loudspeakers

How Loudspeakers Work

A loudspeaker converts electrical signals into sound waves. The main components are:

  • Diaphragm: A thin, flexible membrane that vibrates to produce sound.
  • Voice Coil: A coil of wire that moves back and forth in a magnetic field.
  • Magnet: Creates a magnetic field that interacts with the voice coil.

When an electrical signal is applied to the voice coil, it moves within the magnetic field, causing the diaphragm to vibrate and produce sound.

Where They Come From

Loudspeakers have a rich history dating back to the 1800s. Key figures include:

  • Thomas Edison
  • Edgar Rice Burroughs
  • Henry Kellogg

Timeline of Loudspeaker History

  • 1800s

    First experiments in sound reproduction.

  • 1920s

    Development by Bell, Rice, and Kellogg.

  • 1950s

    Introduction of hi-fi technology.

  • 1960s

    Development of stereo systems.

  • 2000s

    Invention of Bluetooth speakers.

  • 2010s

    Emergence of smart speakers.
